Method, sources and limits

Ridge regression fits ten standardized features: 100 m wind speed, capped speed cubed, cloud cover, surface shortwave radiation, temperature, four daily harmonic terms and intensity 24 hours earlier. A positive penalty stabilizes correlated features. The regularization strength is selected on an earlier chronological validation slice; scaling is learned from training rows only. Forecasts beyond 24 hours feed back their own predictions, not future target values.

The retrospective test freezes the NESO forecast from the source capture shown above. Weather uses an explicitly labelled 48-hour seasonal-persistence forecast because the as-issued weather archive was unavailable during preparation. The forward run uses the weather forecast in its labelled input capture. Regional targets are historical estimates; only the GB target uses national actual intensity. We do not claim an operationally vintaged training archive or causal accuracy.

Wind vectors come from ECMWF IFS at 100 m above ground, sampled on a 100 km grid through Open-Meteo. Tracer positions advance with second-order midpoint integration on the GPU; trails retain recent positions in two alternating framebuffers. The displayed motion is accelerated 1,200 times. Bearings name where wind comes from; tracers move downwind. Wind means are not weighted by turbine capacity and omit offshore sites beyond the DNO areas. Surface sunlight is the API’s preceding-hour shortwave radiation. This is modelled weather, not observed air-parcel paths.

Methods: Hoerl & Kennard (1970), ridge regression; WebGL 2 transform feedback; Open-Meteo ECMWF fields. Weather data are CC BY 4.0; the free API has non-commercial fair-use terms.

How to read this forecast

Colour is regional carbon intensity in grams of CO₂ per kilowatt-hour. The weather underlay shows ECMWF 100 m wind or surface sunlight, separately from the carbon colour and numbers. It does not represent wind direction or electricity moving between regions.

The presenter uses written rules, with no language model. It compares the largest regional improvements and deteriorations with the current source interval, presents wind, solar and gas share changes, and uses the API’s GB aggregate for cleaner-band crossings and the cleanest overnight window (22:00–07:00). Named regions are outlined; national stories outline all 14 regions. The map and timeline move to each caption’s forecast time. Every caption shows its source values, dates and units. Comparisons describe changes in the forecast, rather than their causes. A two-hour mean uses four consecutive half-hours. These are estimates throughout, including the historical part of the timeline.

Forecast frames are hatched. Display values between half-hours are interpolated. The cleanest window always refers to the remaining forecast after the current reference time; in saved mode, that is the saved snapshot time. It is a carbon signal, not a price forecast. Labels use fixed bands: lower below 100, moderate 100–199, higher 200–299 and very high at 300+ gCO₂/kWh.

The map uses approximate DNO licence boundaries, simplified to 650 metres. Its geometry is indicative; it is not a connection-boundary lookup.
